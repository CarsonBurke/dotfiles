# Writing emails

Write emails that give the recipient a clear reason to reply. Keep the message as short as the purpose allows.

## Before drafting

Identify the purpose of the message and the response the sender wants. Use the smallest amount of context needed for the recipient to answer. Do not invent facts, commitments, prices, dates, relationships, or prior conversations.

For outreach, check the sent mailbox before choosing recipients. Remove duplicate recipients and companies that were already contacted unless the user asks for a follow up. Keep the first batch small when the user wants to review messages before broader outreach.

## Style

Use plain, professional language. Sound like a person who wrote the message for this recipient. Use a normal subject that states the purpose.

Avoid sales slogans, inflated claims, filler, generic introductions, forced warmth, and phrases that sound generated. Avoid jargon unless the recipient needs it. Use contractions when they fit the sender's voice. Use complete sentences and short paragraphs.

Do not use em dashes or en dashes. Do not use decorative punctuation, fake quotations, or unexplained abbreviations. Do not overexplain technical details in the first message.

## Outreach structure

Use this order when it fits:

1. Say why you are writing.
2. Ask for one clear action.
3. Give the few facts needed to answer.
4. Ask for the practical details that affect the decision.
5. Close with the sender's name and contact details.

For a quote request, ask for price and availability first. Add condition, lead time, shipping, warranty, and the main configuration only when they affect the quote. Save detailed specifications for the next exchange.

For a service request, state the location, size, expected load, required connectivity, access needs, and timing. Ask the recipient to confirm whether they can support the request and provide pricing.

## Follow ups

A follow up should refer to the earlier message and ask whether the recipient can respond. Do not repeat the entire request. Ask one question, such as whether the request is with the right person or when an answer is likely.

When replying to a recipient, answer their question first. Include new information only when it helps resolve the next decision.

## Sending and review

Drafting does not authorize sending. Before an external send, show the recipients, subject, and final body when approval is required. Send only after the user gives clear approval.

Immediately before sending, check the recipient list against the sent mailbox again. Confirm that the sender address, attachments, links, dates, quantities, and requested action are correct. Do not include secrets or unrelated personal information.

For several recipients, use one message per company when the request needs personalization. Keep a record of the recipient, subject, date sent, and purpose so later follow ups do not create duplicates.
